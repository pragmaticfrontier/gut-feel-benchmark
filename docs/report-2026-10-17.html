Summary

The first run of the Gut Feel Benchmark put 100 items to seven contestants on 17 October 2026: three decision models and three chat models, one of them run twice. OpenAI's Decisions API led with a Gut Score of 59.0, answering in a median 0.30 seconds, and Jev came second at 51.3. Claude Opus 5.5 judged best, with a skill of 71.2, but took 2.0 seconds per answer and scored 32.0.

Decision models answered faster. The best chat models answered better. On a scale that counts an answer only when it beats the deadline, speed set the order.

Reasoning

High reasoning bought Opus no skill. It used 141 reasoning tokens per answer instead of 36, scored 69.9 against 71.2 and slowed from 2.0 to 3.1 seconds, which cost it 6 points of Gut Score.

Reasoning off held for none of the chat models. Sol used 162 reasoning tokens per answer and Gemini 3.8 Flash 2,169, with no warning from the SDK, and Gemini ran past the 120-second limit on 23 of 25 poker hands, so a quarter of its items count as failures.

Method

Each contestant answered the first 25 items of four packs. Poker hands are scored against exact equity over all 1,712,304 boards, and screening-test problems against the exact posterior by Bayes' rule. Cleveland Clinic patients are scored against their angiograms, and Hacker News stories posted from 2 to 9 October 2026 against whether they reached 100 points.

Every item went through AI SDK 7's experimental_decide on Vercel AI Gateway, natively for the decision models and through a structured-output adapter for the chat models. Calls ran four at a time with a 120-second limit and no retries, after one untimed warm-up call per contestant. Gateway answered a request that does no model work in 40 to 56 milliseconds, so network time is a small share of every latency.

No deadline scores above a contestant's skill with no deadline, so a late answer can lower a score but never raise it. The intervals come from 1,000 paired bootstrap resamples of the items, each scored against the pack's own base rate.

Limits

  • Twenty-five items per pack leave the middle of the table unsettled.
  • GPT-6.1 Sol and Gemini 3.8 Flash with high reasoning have not run.
  • The intervals cover which items were drawn, not run-to-run variation in a model's answers or latency.
  • All latencies come from one machine on one evening, so they compare with each other and not with other runs.
  • No human expert has answered yet, although a person can join a run from the terminal.
